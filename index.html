Coordinated Load-Generation Optimization for Renewable Integrated Microgrid

Gayathri K  ·  Pallavi Bharadwaj
Department of Electrical Engineering, Indian Institute of Technology Gandhinagar, Gujarat, India
Paper (IEEE Xplore) Try the interactive demo Cite

In one paragraph

A microgrid with solar PV, a battery, a diesel generator and a grid connection has to keep supply equal to demand at every moment while paying as little as possible for grid power. This work folds load management directly into the cost optimization: loads are split into critical and flexible ones, and flexible loads are shed in priority order only when a shed-penalty term says it is worth it. The result is lower grid cost during expensive hours, uninterrupted supply to critical loads, and — once the diesel generator is removed — near-zero grid import at peak price with no fossil generation.

Coordinated optimization

Generation (PV, BESS, DG, grid) and load are scheduled together to minimise grid import cost.

Priority-based shedding

Critical loads are never shed; flexible loads go in a fixed order, lowest priority first.

Shed penalty

A penalty term in the objective stops the optimizer from cutting load when it is not needed.

Lower emissions

Demand control lets the system ride through peak-price hours without running the diesel generator.

The microgrid

All sources and the load meet at the point of common coupling (PCC). The energy management system (EMS) reads the state of every unit and sends set-points to the battery, the diesel generator, the grid interface and the flexible loads.

Method

1 · Keep supply equal to demand

At every instant the power delivered to the load must equal what the sources provide. The delivered load is the demand minus whatever was shed.

Pld = PPV + PBESS + PDG + Pgrid (1)
Pld = Pload − Pshed (2)

2 · Minimise cost, but charge a price for shedding

Over a 24-hour horizon, the optimizer pays the generation cost CG for grid power and a shed penalty SP for every kW of flexible load it cuts. A weighted battery-energy term rewards keeping energy in storage.

OF = Σt=0T dt · ( CG·Pgrid + Σi=1m SP·Pshed,i ) − W·EBESS (3)

In plain terms: shedding one kW saves CG but costs SP, so a flexible load is cut only in hours when grid power is dearer than its penalty.

3 · Constraints

Pshed,i = 0  for critical loads (4)
0 ≤ Pshed,i(t) ≤ Pload,i(t)  for flexible loads (5)
20% ≤ SOC(t) ≤ 80% (6)
PBESSmin ≤ PBESS(t) ≤ PBESSmax,   EBESSmin ≤ EBESS(t) ≤ EBESSmax (7–8)

4 · Decision flow each time step

① Is there spare solar?

If load < PV, charge the battery or export the surplus to the grid.

② Is diesel cheaper than grid?

If the DG is connected and CDG < Cgrid, the diesel generator runs.

③ Check battery SOC

At SOCmin: import and charge. At SOCmax: discharge. In between: charge or discharge as cost dictates.

④ Optimise the load

In every branch, flexible loads are trimmed during high import cost, lowest priority first.

What the simulations showed

The study was run in MATLAB/Simulink on the microgrid above with one-minute load data over 24 hours, comparing an existing cost-optimal EMS against the proposed one.

Existing EMS

During the evening price peak the diesel generator runs and its surplus is exported to the grid. All load is served.

Proposed EMS

The same peak triggers partial shedding of the AC (load 5) and ceiling fan (load 4). Critical loads stay fully supplied throughout.

Proposed EMS, no diesel

With demand control alone, the microgrid holds grid import close to zero at peak price — no fossil generation needed.
